## Vision

The shipping game is a 2.5D / 3D PC online multiplayer tank game. Players command a small company by voice. Teammates are bots that take those orders the way a human squad would: they understand the intent, pick a sensible route, shoot while moving, and say when the order is done, impossible, or needs a new call.

What exists in this folder is not that game. It is a 2D top-down browser prototype (Vite, TypeScript, HTML canvas, no engine). It exists to answer two questions before anyone spends time on a 3D client:

- **Fun.** Is it more fun to give radio orders than to drive every tank yourself?
- **Feasibility.** Can bots follow natural commands well enough that a match is playable, not a tech demo of a parser?

If both answers stay yes, the thing that ports is the order contract (utterance → goal → route → motor), not the canvas renderer. A 3D client should consume the same goals. It should not relearn "what did the player just say."

Working title in the repo: Fog of War — Tank Hunt. Package name `fog-of-war-tank-hunt`. GitHub remote used in earlier sessions: `HeadandFolders/Tanki`. Multiplayer scripts were developed on branch `cursor/mp-offline-mode-1e16`. Confirm the checked-out branch before assuming main has `dev:mp`.

## How one order runs

Example: the player says "4 attack 2".

1. Speech or the text box produces a string. Spoken number words are normalized (four → 4). The radio gate suppresses same-team/text/addressee repeats for 1500ms and remembers recognizer segment ids. Admitted partial intent gets synchronous standby before awaiting either parser; chatter is silent.
2. The phrase reader splits on the verb. Numbers before attack are friendlies. The number after is the enemy, even without the word "enemy". Other tanks are not retasked.
3. `assignOrder` replaces only the addressed base orders, retaining valid interrupts. Blue tank 4 receives player `attackNearest` with red 2's id and command provenance. The shared acknowledgement is honest about any retreat. No model is required for this sentence.
4. Every frame, both friendly brains advance the same level-triggered stack. Healthy attack holds station and fires inside range or drives toward its one locked prey. Fog routes use LOS or stored team contacts, not hidden live coordinates. Low HP/panic temporarily activates retreat with push/pop captions; resume revalidates the prey.
5. Prey death ends the attack into hold with `prey_dead_hold`; lost belief expires it with `prey_lost` and a last-seen direction from the saved contact. A multi-target order also pins its initial prey; no silent switch to another survivor. Base destruction completes `attackBase`. Death or match end closes outstanding orders.
6. Walls and exposure remain the pathfinder's job. The language model never emits a path. All goal writes, including initializers, replay and network restore, go through the stack helper; readonly goal types and `scripts/check-goal-writes.ts` enforce the boundary.

"Everyone cover me", "2 move south and shoot at will", and "3 take the right and I'll take the left" now have deterministic-reader coverage; do not describe all of them as model-only. Unsettled sentences can still wait on the LLM (timeout 12s) or Needle. Standby covers the wait, not parser quality. Needle remains optional and off by default.

A cloud-backed session requires a successful pre-match probe. Play offline deliberately uses the phrase reader and rejects unresolved guesses with "say again". During this verification the configured probe returned HTTP 401; no real model success is claimed. Do not read, print or commit local credentials just to diagnose that status.

## Decisions already made

Language and motion stay separate. An utterance becomes a Goal. A hand-written brain plus A\* carries the goal out. There is no reinforcement learning in this repo and there never was. Nothing was trained and removed. (A throwaway PettingZoo / MAPPO sandbox was sketched outside this repo while weighing the RL route. It was shelved, and none of it is in the tree. Do not resurrect it.)

RL was skipped because the experiment is "does the order feel obeyed and can a human say why it failed," not "discover superhuman micro." A policy that kites well and ignores "5 attack 6" fails the experiment. A bad route is a grid cost you can draw. A bad policy is a training run. The browser build has no GPU sim farm.

What that choice gives up, on purpose: learned peeking, adaptive kiting against a human, and coordination that emerges instead of being placed in slots. If bots later need to feel like good players, train a motor controller under an already-set Goal (steer, fire, peek, given the goal and local cover). Do not train utterance → joystick. Speech errors, parse errors, and driving errors would become one number, and Janna eval could not tell which failed.

A later review pushed back on a too-clean version of this design. The pushback was largely right. Accept it:

- Slice B names legal deviations in both friendly brains: `panic_melee`, `hp_retreat`, `los_shot_on_path`, `prey_dead_hold`, `prey_lost`, `escort_slot_blocked`. HP retreat applies to ordered movement as well as attacks unless `noRetreat` is explicit. Nearby clear shots do not silently convert a player move into an attack.
- Slice B closes the lifecycle hole: attacks pin a prey and hold/bark on death or belief loss. Move/spread/explicit-retreat/report/debug completions speak, as do unsupported orders and lost escorts. Interrupted attacks retain their base provenance and revalidate before resuming. A wall alone does not imply loss in the no-fog modes; fog contact TTL governs belief loss.
- `routeCommitSec` (0.4s) means "do not re-run A\* every frame." The hull still moves every tick along the polyline, and replans if the next step is blocked, the order changes, or an enemy moves onto the remaining path. What is missing is a fast layer that can leave the polyline for a moment to dodge a shell or a teammate, then snap back. That layer is steering. The cost of RL here is training and eval, not inference speed. Do not repeat "a neural net is too slow to run." *(Updated: build the dodge as a plain rule first, not a network. See Latest decisions.)*
- A danger scalar is not a firing position. "Which cell sees tank 6 and the fewest other guns, and stays in cover until the last tiles" is a search over the occupancy and line-of-sight we already have. Build that query and draw it before anyone imitates peeks.
- `mpOfflineFriendlyBrain` and `friendlyBrain` are already long chains of `if (goal)`. A behavior tree or utility AI only helps after lifecycle states exist (pursue, engage, lost, done, aborted). *(Updated: for v1, implement the semantics on the current brains with a shared `interruptStack` helper. Express the same statuses as behavior-tree nodes only when a second goal type, such as escort plus peek, forces composition.)*
- Stop-and-shoot in the fight band makes a bot a turret against a human. Fine for bot-vs-bot readability. For humans, add hold-time jitter and a strafe that stays on the named target. That is still not a reason to replace the goal layer.
- Five bots and one corner: escort fans an arc, but cells are not reserved, and the slot is the VIP's current position, not where the player is about to be. If `fitBodyStep` is ~0 for several frames, the ally blocking the corridor should become a temporary wall and the route should replan.
- Pronouns ("hold there", "him", "or back to position") and speech-recognition junk are a dialog state problem (last addressee, last place, last enemy) plus a clarify/cancel path. A policy under a parser that guesses will learn the guesses.

`routeCommitSec` in `src/config.ts` is 0.4. Older notes say 0.5. Trust the file.

## Latest decisions (second design review, 2026-11-10)

These supersede the "What to do next" order that was in the earlier version of this file. The principle: **make deviation audible, resumable, and machine-checkable.** Nothing here puts a learned policy under the radio.

### The milestone: "Radio teammate v1"

One session of MP Offline / TDM No Fog. v1 shows an **obedient, honest radio teammate**. It does not show "human instincts"; peek-and-shoot is deferred, so do not present it as that bar.

In scope:

1. Fixed-step replay harness with seeded RNG (even if only 3 scenarios).
2. Goal stack with the conflict rules and hysteresis below.

### Order stack and conflict rules

`Goal` is no longer one slot. `src/interrupt-stack.ts` gives each hull an `InterruptStack`: a base order plus at most one interrupt, both on the same channel as "copy". The TDM low-HP retreat that used to do `tank.goal = { kind: "retreat", source: "system" }` now pushes `{kind, reason, until}` and leaves the player order's provenance intact — the base keeps `source: "player"` and `issuer`, and `tank.goal` is a read-only view of the effective goal written only by the helper (`scripts/check-goal-writes.ts` enforces the single write path inside `npm run eval:replay`). Every order ends in exactly one of: success, interrupted, expired, replaced, each logged as an `OrderEvent` with its reason code.

| Case | Rule | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New player order during a retreat | Replace the base order; keep the interrupt if still valid (HP still low). | Implemented |
| Two interrupts at once | Priority: panic_melee > low_HP > dodge. Dodge is momentary, never a stacked goal. Only one non-dodge interrupt at a time. | Implemented, except `stuck` |
| Resume after an interrupt | Re-validate. Prey dead or out of belief → expire and bark. Otherwise resume. | Implemented |
| Flapping | Hysteresis. Retreat enters at ≤32% HP and exits at ≥45% (or after clear for `panicCommitSec`). No re-push inside the commit window. Panic already had `panicClearRadius` / `panicCommitSec`; retreat got the same treatment. | Implemented |
| Prey dies | Hold and say so. Never silently retarget the nearest enemy. | Implemented |
| Prey lost behind a wall | Bark "lost 6, last seen …", then hold. Do not keep driving at a stale position forever. | Implemented |
| Stuck (`fitBodyStep` ≈ 0 for ~0.4–0.6s) | Reason code `stuck`, bark, force a replan with nearby allies as soft blockers. A freeze must never be a silent `moveX = 0`. | Implemented |

Reason codes now named and logged next to the command record (`OrderReason` in `src/interrupt-stack.ts`): `panic_melee`, `hp_retreat`, `los_shot_on_path`, `prey_dead_hold` (the old `prey_dead_retarget` — retargeting was removed, so the code became the hold it describes), `prey_lost`, `escort_slot_blocked`, `stuck`, plus lifecycle codes `order_received`, `order_replaced`, `order_complete`, `order_unsupported` and end-of-life codes `escort_lost`, `tank_destroyed`, `match_ended`.

### Radio and barks

Barks are the player-facing rules of engagement: reason codes are for us, barks are what the human hears. Slice B shipped this — an order is no longer one ack followed by silence, and the low-HP retreat speaks.

- Every push and pop of the stack fires the same channel as "copy" (`emit()` in `src/interrupt-stack.ts`). Examples as they appear: `#5 copy, attacking 6`; `#5 falling back, low HP`; `#5 target down, holding`; `#5 lost 6, last seen north; holding`.
- **Captioned radio, not spoken.** Text only — no earcon and no TTS was added. Radio is continuous speech recognition, so bot speech into an open mic would generate junk; if TTS ever lands it must mute recognition for the bark's duration. Barks land in two places: the thin "Team radio" strip in the sidebar (`index.html` `.radio-strip`, `#chat`, ~5.5em max height, `aria-live="polite"`) and `#radio-overlay` over the bottom of the map (last three captions, ~8s, `pointer-events:none`, `aria-hidden` because the sidebar log is the accessible copy). The overlay exists because below 900px CSS width the layout stacks and the sidebar is off-screen while you play — without it, barks had nowhere visible to land exactly where the player is looking.
- **No flooding.** One company line when the parse is shared — `Company: 3 copy, attacking 6` (game.ts coalesces accepts into one `Company:` chat entry). Per-hull audible barks are limited to high priority: HP/panic interrupts, target loss, deviations, and order endings (`tank_destroyed`, `match_ended`, `escort_lost`); completions only speak for `source: "player"` bases, so commander-authored traffic stays quiet. `stuck` will join this list in Slice C. Routine copies rate-limit to one per tank per 2s (`stack.lastCopyAt`) and `assignOrder` stays silent when the shared parse already spoke.
- **Standby.** `Company: standby` is captioned synchronously from the intake path — `beginUtterance()` fires before the parser awaits, so it lands well inside 300ms even when Needle or the LLM takes seconds (`src/radio.ts`, `src/main.ts`).
- **Idempotent repeats.** The same normalized utterance to the same addressees within 1500ms does not push a second time, and recognizer segment ids are deduped alongside the text; both windows are per world and per team and are cleared by a failure or an order expiry.
- **Say again.** A failed or junk parse captions `Company: say again` and clears the dedupe so the retry lands; `orderAcknowledgement` answers "negative, low HP" / "negative, close threat" / "negative, order unsupported in this mode" instead of a false copy. No silent best-effort attack on the commander.
- **Barks read belief, not god-view.** In clear/MP, line of sight through an open corridor is fair knowledge. Through fog, "last seen north" comes from `teamBelief` / last contact, never live enemy coordinates — enforced by assigning orders on the commander/proxy `view` and by the fog-gated `routeFoes` danger field; `eval-replay` and `eval-radio` assert it.

### Perception fairness and dodge

- Enemy tanks today are `liveVisibleEnemies` = alive + `canSeeTank` (wall line of sight). In TDM No Fog / MP, share is `clear`, with no reaction delay.
- Projectiles are **not in the AI at all** today. `updateProjectiles` is physics only. A 0ms dodge on `world.projectiles` would be aimbot-adjacent next to a 200–250ms human.
- Fog modes still give gunners team-belief contacts the player may not see (`report` feeds all blues into belief).
- When dodge ships it needs: line of sight to the round (or its tracer), a rolled delay of about 180–280ms, then the steer. The same delay applies to "enemy appeared."
- Dodge starts as a **rule**, not an MLP: if a perceived projectile will hit this hull and the cell perpendicular to velocity is free and not a friendly, step there; otherwise stay on the committed path. Randomize left/right, skip about 15% of the time, skip if holding (unless panic), and reject the step if the danger field there is worse than the current cell. Learn something only if this fails on cluttered corners.
- Headless dodge tests: round on collision course with a wall-free side → survived; blocked side → no warp through brick.

### Metrics, scoring, and other settled points

- Slice C metrics collected on `CommandRecord`: `t_ack_ms` (intake → first acknowledgement caption, wall-clock ms; null when the 2s rate limit stayed silent), `repeat_count` (repeats of the same normalized utterance+addressees swallowed by the 1500ms dedupe gate), `interrupts` (goal interrupts attached to that command), `died_stuck` (1 when an addressee was destroyed with its stuck flag on). Persisted to localStorage `tanki-command-postmortem-v1` and shown as a compact suffix on each Janna panel row plus the Shift+dump JSON table.
- Walls are not destructible in play, so the tactical query may cache line of sight until a map-editor commit or a mode restart. If destructible brick is ever added, invalidate on wall-set change, not per shot. Do not design for a mechanic that does not exist.
- Imitation learning stays last, and only with the Goal that was active recorded alongside each demonstration. The player's own hull has no Goal, so demos need labeling. A small self-play set is the same unseen-case brittleness that made RL the wrong first tool.
- Language: the semantics (stack, priorities, resume, hysteresis, barks) are the product. The behavior tree is how you keep them from rotting. Do not block "audible, resumable, checkable" on a full AI rewrite.

## Tank types (2026-11-12)

## Slow respawning (2026-11-12)

Dead hulls come back. No middle zone, no rate that moves yet — the point of this step is the clock, the return, and the one function the next step will bend.

**Two numbers, one hook.** `RESPAWN = { delaySec: 25, protectionSec: 2 }` and `getRespawnRate(team)` live in `src/config/balance.ts` with every other gameplay number. `getRespawnRate` returns 1.0 for both sides and is called from exactly one place — `updateRespawns` multiplies `dt` by it — so zone control arrives by changing that function and nothing else. The rate is the only scale; the delay stays `RESPAWN.delaySec`.

**Where it applies.** `isMpRules` only: Base War, Multiplayer · Offline, Multiplayer · Online. TDM / CTF / Heavy-Light still leave a wreck for good, so their wipe-and-wreck victories and their fixtures are untouched. The timer is armed in `applyDamage` — the single hull-damage choke point — and nowhere else, which is what keeps every harness that sets `alive = false` by hand honest: a hull killed without going through damage has no countdown and stays dead.

**The return.** `respawnTank` in `src/game.ts` revives the same `Tank` object — same id, role and number — at `tank.spawnPos` with full HP, its team's spawn heading, `cooldown` 0, the nuke round warming up again from `nuke.warmupSec`, all order bookkeeping (route, drive track, stuck flag, report/debug progress, `killedBy`) cleared, and the order it was following dropped: it holds position at base until someone names a new one. `spawnPos` is written once by `spawnTeam` (`src/types.ts` gained three optional fields — `spawnPos`, `respawnLeft`, `spawnProtectionUntil` — optional so the hand-built Tank literals in `render.ts` and `ai-commander.ts` still compile).

**Protection is a plain timer.** For `RESPAWN.protectionSec` after the hull appears, `applyDamage` returns before armour is read, so shells and the nuke blast do it no damage. Nothing cancels it early — not firing, not moving, not entering a base's gun line. Structures are unaffected: bases take damage through `applyBaseDamage`, which never sees the flag.

**Victory rules survive, honestly.** `checkOutcome` still ends an MP match on a wipe, but a wipe now means the whole enemy company dead in the same instant; killing them one at a time no longer finishes anything. Base War was already base-only, and its comment that "respawns are the next step" is now true.

**HUD.** `#respawn-hud` in the top bar (beside `#base-hp`) lists each of the local team's waiting hulls as `#3 NV 17s`, counting down, and hides when nobody is in the ground. It reads the exported selector `respawnQueue(world, team)` from `src/game.ts`; the per-frame path in `src/main.ts` never touches sim internals, and `sim/` stays DOM-free.

**Netcode.** `respawnLeft` is carried in `NetTankSnap` (`src/net/protocol.ts`, encoded/decoded in `src/net/snapshot.ts`) so a guest can draw its own countdown; the host is authoritative and `spawnProtectionUntil` is not sent. **Batch metrics.** `src/sim/match.ts` counts respawns (dead → alive transitions, symmetric with the existing kill detection) and, every tick, the seconds each team has fewer than `spec.underStrengthAlive` living hulls. ## Contested middle zone (2026-11-12)

The seam the previous step left is now filled: a circle in the middle of the map decides which team's respawn clock runs fast. One rule, one function, no other effect.

**The rule, in numbers.** `MIDDLE_ZONE = { centerFraction: {x: 0.5, y: 0.5}, radiusFraction: 0.18, holdRate: 2, baseRate: 1 }` in `src/config/balance.ts`. The radius is a fraction of map *height* so the 80×50 and the 144×90 arenas get the same relative footprint (~6.4% of the map area on both) and the same reach from either base; `middleZoneGeometry(width, height)` resolves it to world px at `createWorld` time, after the walls have set the extent. Sized by measurement, not by guess: a throwaway probe over generated maps showed the skeleton keeps the centre open (54-72% of tank-fit samples land inside at this radius) and both spawn sets sit exactly equidistant from it (1115px large, 301px dense), so no side is favoured by the geometry.

**One hook, one caller.** `getRespawnRate(team, zone?)` is still the only place a clock is scaled and `updateRespawns` is still its only caller — the signature just gained the control pair. The rate is not derived twice: `updateMiddleZone` in `src/game.ts` counts living hulls inside the circle and writes `holder` (`null` when contested) plus `contested` onto `world.middleZone`, and `getRespawnRate` pays the bonus when and only when `zone.holder === team`. Because `holder` is a team flag, "two tanks in the zone" is not expressible — it cannot stack, which is also what the rule asks for. It runs at the top of the tick, above `updateRespawns`, so the countdown uses control decided in the same tick.

**No other effects.** Nothing about vision, damage, capture, routing or doctrine reads the zone. Outside `isMpRules` nothing respawns, so a hold would be a bonus that cannot exist: the canvas draws the ring in the open palette and the HUD strip hides rather than advertising it.

**Drawing.** `src/render.ts` gained `zonePalette`, `drawMiddleZoneFill` and `drawMiddleZoneRing` — a ground wash drawn with the home tint (under walls and hulls) and a dashed 2px ring plus a centre pip drawn outside the camera transform (over them, so the boundary is never lost behind brick), in blue `#e8c040` / red `#ec5555` / contested `#b45ad0` / open `#8f9aa8`, i.e. the two team canvas colours. `renderMinimap` circles it too, at `Math.max(3, radius × scale)`. Colours live in `CONFIG.colors`, not inline.

**HUD.** `#zone-hud` sits beside `#respawn-hud` in the top bar and reads `middle blue holds · respawn blue ×2 red ×1`; the class on the state span carries the colour. `renderMiddleZone(world)` in `src/main.ts` is the only reader.

**Netcode.** `NetStateSnap` gained `middleHolder` / `middleContested`; `worldToSnap` emits and `applySnap` assigns them. Control is the host's call — the guest only carries it to the HUD, and no guest code reads it for anything else. The replay snapshot needed no schema work (`captureReplaySnapshot` structured-clones the world).

**Metrics.** `MatchRecord` gained `zoneHoldSeconds` per team and `zoneContestedSeconds`, accumulated per tick after the existing per-team loop; `summarize` exposes `zone.hold`, `zone.contestedShareMean` and `zone.openShareMean` (the remainder, so the four shares sum to 100%). `scripts/sim-matches.ts` prints a MIDDLE ZONE CONTROL table under the header line that states the rule, and says so out loud when the mode is not an MP ruleset.

## Seeded map generator (2026-11-12)

Asked for: maps from a seed instead of a hand-authored layout — about 3× the current area sized so base-to-base costs 35-45s at average hull speed, 180° rotationally symmetric with the bases on opposite walls rather than in corners, at least three distinct routes (short and exposed, longer and covered, one through the middle zone) with A\* verifying each one and verifying no straight shot between the bases, walls that break sightlines and force detours with the distinctness rule written down and a failing candidate re-seeded, a `--map-seed` flag, a debug overlay of the three routes, and a 20-seed report of success rate, route lengths and win rate by side per map.

**Where it lives.** `src/mapgen.ts` is the generator and its own auditor. Every number is in `MAP_GEN` in `src/config/balance.ts` (AGENTS.md's one file): `MAP_GEN.size` (area multiplier and the 35-45s window), `MAP_GEN.layout` (corridor widths, baffle pitch, bay counts), `MAP_GEN.sight` (what counts as exposed), `MAP_GEN.rules` (what makes a candidate fail) and `MAP_GEN.baseToBaseSec`. `chooseMapSize()` picks the shape and `MAP_GEN_SIZE` freezes it; `describeMapSize()` prints the estimate. `generatedBaseTile()` and `generatedApronTiles()` are shared by the generator, the Base War placement and the spawn box, so nobody keeps a private copy of where a base sits. The arena is `ArenaId = "generated"`, selectable in the menu's Arena pick, and `buildArena` gives it a branch that skips `ensureTwoRoutes` and the saved wall edit — the generator's own A\* pass is what says the map has three routes, and a repair pass afterwards would move the ground it measured.

**The size it chose.** 204×190 tiles (3264×3040px), 2.99× the 12960-tile large reference. Base-to-base straight 3152px, route estimate 4539px, **40.0s at the 113.3px/s average hull** (`averageHullSpeed` over `LOADOUTS.company`), inside the 35-45s window. Bases sit mid-wall on the west and east borders.

**The layout.** The field starts solid and routes are carved, always in mirrored pairs through `setPair`, so symmetry is a construction property rather than something checked afterwards: an avenue of two straight arms either side of the wall band between the bases (the short, exposed way), a baffle-lined alley outside it (the long, covered way), a wave straddling the band (the middle-zone way), and alcoves with one mouth each for the rest. The alley's baffles are the load-bearing part and their dimensions are forced by two constraints that meet in the middle: alternation must be drivable by a hull on the tank-fit lattice — a diagonal only exists when both orthogonal corner cells are stances too — while no unbroken firing line may run along a flank. That is `alleyRows 6, baffleTiles 2, baffleDepthTiles 3, baffleEveryTiles 5`; pitch 3 and 4 sever the alley for a hull and pitch 6 opens a firing line.

**What makes a candidate fail** (`MAP_GEN.rules`, all measured, none assumed): the short route must be ≥50% exposed and the covered one ≤25% with a ≥30-point gap, so the map really offers a choice; the shortest drive must be ≥1.1× the base-to-base straight line, so walls force a detour; the covered route must be 1.15-2.2× the short one and the zone route ≤2.2×, so neither is a slog nobody takes; ≥90% of the tank-fit lattice must be reachable from a base; and no route may run alongside another — at most **12% of one route's tiles within 3 tiles of another route's**, outside the 21-tile merge radius where every lane leaves through the same apron. The last rule replaced an earlier minimum-closest-approach rule, which is unsatisfiable on this layout: the wave has to cross the trunks to get anywhere, so a distance test rejected every map for a handful of legitimate crossing tiles. A candidate failing any rule is thrown away and rebuilt from `seed + attempt × 7919`, up to 24 attempts.

**Verification — generation.** `npm run eval:mapgen -- 20` (seeds 20261112-31): **20 accepted, 100% first-chain success, 1.15 attempts per map, ~500ms per map, 20/20 read back as exact 180° mirrors, 20/20 carrying all three routes, no rejects.** Route means over those seeds: direct 294 tiles / 41.5s / 100% open; protected 431 tiles / 60.8s / 19% open; zone 421 tiles / 59.5s / 100% open and through the zone. `generatedMapIsSymmetric` re-rasterises the accepted wall list and compares every tile with its partner, so the check tests what the game actually loads rather than the generator's internal grid.

**Verification — the rest of the tree.** `npx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.json` clean. `eval:replay` **46/46** (the mandated seeded fixed-step harness), `check-goal-writes` clean, `eval:orders` 41/41, `eval:radio` 29/29, `eval:stuck` 18/18, `eval-commander --quick` 17/0, `eval-basewar --quick` 13 checks. Nothing about the existing arenas changed: `CONFIG.arenaDefault` is still `large`, and `?map-seed` / `--map-seed` default to the seed the large map already used, so an unmodified run is bit-identical to before.

**Playing on it.** `npm run sim -- --map generated --map-seed 1001` plays the batch runner on one generated map; `MatchSpec.mapSeed` carries it, the header prints it and the JSON records it. `npm run sim:mapsweep -- --seeds 20` walks map seeds and prints, per map, the three measured route times, the win tally, the share of each base knocked off, the kill tally, the base damage skew and a verdict that says who took a base and in how many seconds, then the pooled win rate with its binomial chance, how many matches ended with a base destroyed and which maps were flagged. In the browser: Arena → Generated, or `http://127.0.0.1:4847/?arena=generated&map-seed=1001&routes=1`, with **R** toggling the three-route overlay (polyline per route, blue-base end ringed, plus a legend of seconds / tiles / % open). `npm run eval:mapgen -- 3 --svg` writes `logs/mapgen-<seed>.svg` dumps with the same overlay if you want the whole map at once.

**Sweep result (20 maps × 1 match, 600s base-war clock, 30Hz, tactical vs tactical, `logs/map-sweep-20x1.json`, 66 min of compute at 200s per match):** **pooled wins blue 10 / red 10, chance 1.000** — no seed where one side wins more often than chance explains, which is also the strongest statement the sample can make, since one match per map has no win rate to test. 19 of the 20 matches ended with a base destroyed (fastest 275s, slowest 568s, median 377s, mean 391s) and the twentieth ran the clock out (seed 20261131, blue ahead 45% to 11% on base damage). Exactly one map is flagged — that clock-decided one — and it is the case the rule was written for: no base fell, both sides past 5%, one side over 1.6× the other. Blue's nine base kills averaged 377s against red's ten at 403s, well inside the 275-568s spread of the set and far too few matches to call a tilt. The route columns repeat on every seed (drive 41.5s, direct 41.5s, protected 60.7-61.2s, zone 59.5s), which is the frozen-geometry item below rather than a fault in the sweep.

**Brittle / open:**

- **The three routes barely move between seeds.** Direct is 294.1 tiles on all twenty, zone 421.1, protected 430-431. The corridors sit at row/column offsets that are fixed fractions of the map, so A\* finds the same lanes every time and the seed changes the cover along them (protected exposure does range 16-25%) rather than the shape. Distinctness is satisfied — the three ways differ in length, exposure and whether they cross the zone — but per-seed *variety* is not, and fixing that means seeding the avenue/trunk row positions, not tuning a rule.
- **Two of the three routes are 100% open.** The rules only require the short one to be exposed and the covered one to be shielded, so the zone route being fully in the daylight is allowed. It reads as intended (the middle zone is open ground by definition) but it is not a third *flavour*, it is the same flavour as the direct route with a detour.
- **A generated map costs about 6× the compute per second of match.** 68 sim steps/s against the large map's 437, because it carries 510 wall rects to large's 132 and hit tests scan them. Measured on the sweep: 20 matches averaging 391s of simulation took 3996s of wall time, i.e. ~200s per match and ~5 min for one that runs the full 600s clock — which is why the sweep is one match per seed and not eight. A spatial index over walls would fix it and is not in this slice.
- **The batch clock no longer decides Base War on a map this big.** At `CONFIG.sim.maxLengthSec` (300s) all six pilot matches ended over-length with the defender's base still standing (one reached 7%); the sweep therefore raises the cap to the engine's own 600s, and the 20-map run proves that was the right call — a base fell in 19 of the 20 matches, at 275-568s with a median of 377s. So 300s-batch balance numbers measured on `large` are not comparable with this map and would have to be re-measured.
- **One match per seed cannot flag a lopsided map by wins**, and the damage ratio cannot do it instead: a base that falls sits at 0% and the side that dropped it at 100% by definition, so an early version of the sweep flagged every decided match (skew 20-54:1) on a number that carried no information. The rule now on disk flags a map only for a win split with `--matches 4` or more, or for a damage split **when no base fell** and both sides got past 5% of one, and the per-map verdict column reports who took a base and in how many seconds. What is left is the pooled win rate over the 20 maps, and at 20 × 1 that came out **10-10** with a single flag, on the one map the clock decided. That is the honest limit here, and it is not a shortfall of the generator: every map is its own 180° rotation, which `eval:mapgen` re-checks on the accepted wall list, so terrain cannot make one base easier to reach than the other and a flagged row can only be the matchup, never the map.
- **No human has seen the overlay yet, but it is proven at pixel level.** `NATIVE_BROWSER_VIEWPORT_UNAVAILABLE` still holds — the in-app surface is hidden, so `take_screenshot` gives no picture and `requestAnimationFrame` never fires, meaning the page never paints by itself. What was checked instead: a fresh load of `?arena=generated&map-seed=1001&routes=1` lands the selects on `mp-offline` / `generated` with 515 walls and 12 tanks in the world and the only console error being the known LLM 401, and a forced synchronous `renderWorld` into the live 1280×800 canvas paints the three route colours where the same camera with `routes = null` paints none — 203 / 100 / 1016 pixels (direct / protected / zone) at the blue base, 100 / 148 / 187 at the east end. 100 of each is the legend's own three swatches, so everything above that is a polyline; the protected route reads only at the east camera because the later-drawn zone route lies on top of it at the start. What still needs a real window is whether it is *legible*, not whether it draws. (A reading of `large` from the arena select during this check was Chrome restoring form values across `location.reload()`, not the app ignoring the query.)
- **The arena-pinned harnesses still cover `dense|large` only.** `eval-commander`'s push-timing guard and `eval-basewar`'s defence guard do not look at `generated`, so nothing in the tree claims the bots behave on it beyond "a match runs, respawns, damages a base and ends".

## One round in the air (2026-11-12)

Asked for: each pellet travels as long as nothing stops it, and a hull cannot fire again while the round it just fired is still in the air.

**The flight.** `Projectile` lost its `life` field and `updateProjectiles` lost the `life -= dt` expiry, so a round leaves the field by hitting a hull, a base or a wall and by nothing else. `projectileLifeOf` and `PROJECTILE_LIFE_SLACK` went with it, which changes what `range` means: it is now purely the gun's *aiming* reach — what a bot will open fire at, what `standoff` and `baseShotClear` measure — and no longer a distance a shell can physically cross. The nuke round is under the same rule and detonates where it collides instead of at the end of a timed throw; for a bot launch, which needs the base inside `nuke.range` with a clear line before it commits, that is the same ~2s flight it always had.

**The gate.** `hasRoundInAir(projectiles, tankId)` in `src/config/balance.ts`, called once at the top of `tryShoot` in `src/game.ts`, so it covers the deck gun and the nuke launch, the human's held Space and the bot's `wantShoot`. It keys on `ownerId` — per hull, not per team, not global — and `notePathShot` in `src/ai.ts` asks the same question, so a caption cannot promise a shot the hull may not take. `fireCooldownOf` still runs alongside it; the new rule is a second and usually longer wait.

**Nothing escapes.** Every arena is ringed by a solid 2-tile stone border and `main.ts` clamps `dt` to 0.05s, so even the fastest shell (480 px/s) steps 24px into a 32px wall. As a backstop, a round found outside the world rect is dropped — a nuke detonates — rather than left to fly forever, because an immortal round would keep its hull disarmed for the rest of the match. Harnesses that strip `world.walls` are exactly where that bites.

**This halves sustained damage, and the number to look at is time of flight.** The cycle is now `max(reload, flight)`, and at the ranges this game fights at the flight wins: a gunner at the 160px standoff Base War measures has a 0.16s reload and a ~0.30s flight. `eval-basewar`'s continuous-firing guard went to **97.07s for one gunner / 32.53s for three** from 56-63s / 18-22s; its windows and its name were re-cut to the new rule, since what it encodes is the mechanic and not a balance target. If that reads too slow in play the knob is `projectileSpeed` or `fireRate` in `TANK_TYPES`, not the rule.

**Netcode.** `NetProjSnap` lost `life` — there is nothing left to count down. The host is authoritative, so the gate runs only there; a guest's reconstructed rounds carry synthetic `ownerId`s and never fire.

**Verification.** `npx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.json` clean. `eval:replay` **48/48**, up from 46/46, on a fixture that holds the trigger and fires south down an empty column: `a round outlives its own gun reach and is spent only by what it hits` (still flying past twice its 175px gun reach, gone inside the border band) and `a hull holds one round in the air and its mate fires on through` (exactly one shot event across the 29 steps that cover its reload while a second hull's round is airborne beside it, then the second shot the tick the first round hits). ## Turrets point where they shoot (2026-11-13)

Asked for: the turret on each tank faces the direction it is shooting at.

## Shooting costs speed (2026-11-13)

Asked for: tanks move at maximum speed when they are not shooting, and slow down when they are.

**The rule.** `FIRE_SLOWDOWN` in `src/config/balance.ts`: `factor` 0.55 while braced, `windowSec` 1.5s. A hull is braced when `world.time - tank.lastShotAt <= windowSec`, and `driveSpeedOf(hull, time)` returns either its table `speed` or `speed × factor`. No stat in `TANK_TYPES` moved, and the window is measured off `lastShotAt`, a field that already existed for the shot event in the replay recorder — so a hull is braced by what it actually fired, not by what it is aiming at or what its brain intended.

**Four read points, one function.** `updatePlayer` in `src/game.ts` (the human's arrow keys) and the three brains in `src/ai.ts` — `mpOfflineFriendlyBrain`, `friendlyBrain`, `enemyBrain` — each used to grab `TANK_TYPES[role].speed` once at the top and hand that number down to `steerTo`, `driveOrder` and `combatManeuver`. Those are the only places a drive speed is chosen, so the multiplier propagates through path-following, escort sprint (`speed * sprint`), the home-return dash (`speed * 1.1`) and the difficulty tier (`tier.speedMul`) without touching any of them. `motion.ts` still knows nothing about guns: it receives a speed, as before.

**The brace starts the frame after the shot.** Both the human and the bots move before `tryShoot` sets `lastShotAt` (`stepWorld` runs the brain, then fires what the brain asked for; `updatePlayer` moves on the keys, then shoots), so the frame a round leaves the gun is still a full-speed frame. That is one frame at 30-60Hz and it keeps the motor out of the firing decision.

**Why a fixed beat and not the gun's own cycle.** Since the one-round-in-the-air rule, a hull's real firing period is `max(reload, time of flight)`, and the flight depends on what it shot at and where the round lands. `1 / fireRate` would have read a gunner as braced for 0.16s between shots that actually take half a second to land, so it would flicker; a window of 1.5s covers a sustained exchange for every hull in the table and lets a hull that shot once and drove off get its speed back inside two seconds. `windowSec` is the knob if that reads as either too sticky or too soft.

**What it costs in px/s, at the table's own numbers.** Scout 165 → 90.8, Gunner 130 → 71.5, Engineer 118 → 64.9, Nuke carrier 82 → 45.1, Heavy 78 → 42.9, Artillery 72 → 39.6. The scout is the one to watch: braced it is slower than a heavy cruising at full tilt, and "kite" is its doctrine — it wants distance and now pays for the shot that buys it. The heavies and artillery barely notice on paper because they were already the slow hulls, which is the other edge of the same rule.

**Measured in play, not on paper.** A 120s headless `mp-offline` match on `large` (seed 1, one world, every living hull sampled each step, averaged over the seconds it was actually moving): scout 67.8 → 47.7 px/s clean-to-braced, engineer 73.0 → 43.1, artillery 48.1 → 34.4, heavy 45.5 → 36.4, nuke carrier 52.4 → 51.5. Every type is slower while braced, which is the mechanic; the ratios sit above 0.55 because both buckets contain turning, holding and arrival, not just cruise, and the nuke row is nearly flat because that hull is standing still when it shoots anyway.

**A batch, with no balance claim attached.** Six `mp-offline` matches, seed 1, `large`, tactical-vs-tactical, 30Hz: with the slowdown live **blue 63 / red 52** kills; the same six with `FIRE_SLOWDOWN.factor` forced to 1 (the old behavior) **blue 39 / red 47**. Both runs ended all six at the 300s engine clock with 0 over-length draws, so nothing froze. The kill ledger moved and the leading side flipped on a six-match sample — that is noise-grade evidence about a rule that changes who can disengage, and `logs/sim-factor1-6mx1.json` (git-ignored) is the baseline run. No conclusion is being drawn.

**Harness edited, and why.** `eval-motion`'s `runMove` budgeted its lane as `800 / speed + 2` seconds, i.e. "800px at top speed plus two seconds of slack". The `enemyOffset: 100` case is precisely a hull that shoots at something standing in its lane, so it stopped arriving inside the budget and failed `enemy move dist 52.6`. The budget now reads `800 / (speed * FIRE_SLOWDOWN.factor) + 2`. Only that one line and its import changed: every other assertion in the block (no reverse, no detour off the lane, fired while passing, shot taken on the move, hold at the arrival point) passes unedited, which is the confirmation that the run needed time and not a fix.

**Verification.** `npx tsc --noEmit` clean. `eval:replay` **48/48**. `eval-orders` 41/41, `eval-stuck` 18/18, `eval-routes` all ok, `eval-motion` ok, `eval-basewar --quick` 13 checks with its continuous-firing guard unchanged at **97.07s / 32.53s**, `eval-commander --quick` 17/0. The push guard was A/B'd directly: with `factor` forced to 1 it reports dense 26.6s / large 71.8s against its 30s / 75s limits, and with `factor` 0.55 the numbers are byte-identical, because that script measures the drive before anything is in range to shoot at. Config restored after each of those two runs (`git diff` on `balance.ts` shows the intended block only).

**Brittle / open:**

- **A braced hull can now read as standing still.** `stepWorld` calls a hull stationary when it moved less than `CONFIG.debugStandStillSpeed * dt + 0.5` this step — 0.7px at 60Hz, i.e. 42 px/s. A braced artillery cruises at 39.6 px/s and an engineer at 64.9 only clears it by steering cleanly, so in TDM / CTF a hull creeping through a firefight beside a marked wreck can bank debug progress it would previously have had to stop for. The threshold predates this change and is not tuned for it; MP modes skip debug entirely, which is why no harness caught it.
- **The window does not know what it is shooting at.** One shell at a target that runs away leaves the hull braced for 1.5s while it drives an empty lane. If that reads as sluggish, `windowSec` is one number, and the alternative — braced only while a target is in reach — would make the rule read as a targeting penalty instead of a firing one.
- **The human hull is now slower while holding Space,** which is exactly what was asked for and exactly what has not been felt. `NATIVE_BROWSER_VIEWPORT_UNAVAILABLE` again: the numbers above come from the fixed-step sim and from sampled headless matches, not from a keyboard. ## The shot lands the tick the round does (2026-11-13)

Asked for: no waiting period between a shell colliding and the next shell firing — the next round should go the moment the previous one lands.

**What the wait actually was — two different things wearing one name.** Measured before touching anything, at 0.6× gun reach against a pinned dummy, 60Hz: gunner 0.017s, scout 0.767s, engineer 0.967s, nuke carrier 1.243s, heavy 1.650s, artillery 5.617s. The big five are not a lag — that is their `fireRate` reload still counting down after a short flight, and removing it is a 5-10× damage change, which was offered and declined. The gunner's 0.017s is the one this step removes: a real ordering artifact, exactly one simulation tick, and it only ever shows on a hull whose round outlives its reload.

**Why a tick existed.** `stepWorld` acts the hulls first (cooldown, brain or player input, `tryShoot`) and resolves `updateProjectiles` later in the same step. So a round collided at the end of step N and its owner's next attempt had already happened at the start of step N — the shot could only land in step N+1. The fix is not a reordering: moving `updateProjectiles` above the hull loop would also move who dies before acting, and hit tests would run against last step's hull positions.

**The retry.** `stepWorld` collects `ShotRequest`s — every hull that asked to fire this step, from `updatePlayer` (both the human and the guest seat) and from the bot branch's `armed` — and after `updateProjectiles` calls `tryShoot` again for each. No gate is bypassed, because `tryShoot` is the same function with the same checks: a hull that already fired this step has a fresh `cooldown` (always > 0, since `fireCooldownOf` is `1 / fireRate`) and now a fresh round in the air, so it cannot fire twice; a hull that died in the collision returns at its own `alive` check; a hull still reloading returns at `cooldown > 0`; a bot with nothing in reach fails the AI sight gate. The aim is the one the hull already chose this step, one tick stale by construction — re-aiming would mean running the brain twice.

**Measured after.** Gunner gap **0.000s** at 30Hz and 60Hz, both from the keyboard seat and from the bot brain (the bot variant needed an order and a pinned target, and its cycle is identical). Every other row matches the before table to the millisecond, which is the point: the change is confined to flight-limited cycles. A gunner's cycle at 132px is 0.217s → 0.200s of measured hit-to-hit accounting, i.e. one shell per ~1.6% more sustained fire at that range, and nothing else moves.

**Verification.** `npx tsc --noEmit` clean. `eval:replay` **48/48** — including `a hull holds one round in the air and its mate fires on through`, which is the check that would catch a double-fire, and `a round outlives its own gun reach and is spent only by what it hits`. `eval-basewar --quick` 13 checks, pace guard printed **97.07s / 32.53s**, identical to before; `eval-commander --quick` 17/0 including "one-survivor adjudication stops inside a step, before a second lethal projectile can wipe red". No harness needed editing this time.

**Brittle / open:**

- **The basewar pace guard did not move, and I do not fully trust that as evidence of nothing.** Its gunners stand 160px out (flight 0.333s) at 30Hz, which is longer than their 0.16s reload, so I expected the cycle to shorten by one tick per shell and the 97s to drop a second or two. It printed the same number to the hundredth. Either the approach leg dominates the shell cadence there or the fixture's hit times are not what the comment says; the probe number is the one to believe about the mechanic, and that guard is the one to re-cut if someone ever tunes `fireRate` again.
- **A freed gun fires at the aim the hull already had.** At 60Hz a scout closing at 165 px/s moves 2.75px per tick, so a round that lands on a moving target's last step shoots at where it was one step ago. That is smaller than the shell's own spread of outcomes and no harness measures it, but it is a real difference from "re-aim, then fire".
- **One deferred attempt per hull per step, not a queue.** A hull that asked to fire, was blocked, and whose round did not land this step simply asks again next step. Nothing is carried between steps, so there is no state to leak across worlds or restores — but it also means the retry is a second `tryShoot` call for every shooting hull every tick, which is cheap today and would not be cheap with a per-shot cost added to that function later.
- **The reload is still the floor, and now it is the whole story.** After this step, the only wait between a hit and the next shell is `fireRate`, so `max(reload, flight)` from "One round in the air" is exact rather than "that plus a tick". Anyone reading the old section's cycle numbers should subtract one step.
- **Still nobody has felt it.** `NATIVE_BROWSER_VIEWPORT_UNAVAILABLE` for the third entry running: 17ms at 60Hz is below what a human can see anyway, so this is a correctness fix dressed as a feel request, and the honest test is the recorded cycle, not a playtest.


Checks that exist (scripts are outside tsc; run with tsx):

- `npm run eval:orders` — goal schema and offline gold sentences. This is `npm test`.

- `npm run eval:radio` — 29 checks: 20 scripted utterances with zero silent deviations or retargets, dedupe, failure reset, silent chatter, synchronous standby under a delayed fake parser, coalesced and rate-limited copies, order replacements that always speak, and world-local command records.

Exit code is non-zero if any seed is rejected, any map is not a mirror, or any map measures fewer than three routes.
- `npm run sim:mapsweep -- --seeds 20` — fairness sweep over generated maps: one full Base War match per map seed (more with `--matches`), printing per map the measured route times, the win tally, the share of each base knocked off, the kill split, the damage skew and a verdict, then the pooled win rate with its binomial chance and how many maps skewed to each side. A map is flagged when the win split is under `--p` (default 5%) chance, or, when the clock decides before a base does, when one side dealt over `--skew` (default 1.6×) the other's base damage. Exit code 2 if anything is flagged; writes the same rows to `logs/map-sweep-<seeds>x<matches>.json`. At ~5 minutes per match on a 3× map, 20×1 is roughly an hour and a half.

`tsconfig` includes `src` only, `noUnusedLocals` on. Do not reference `process` from `src`. PowerShell has no `&&`. On this machine the sandbox often cannot see the workspace; shell commands need full permissions. npm may warn `Unknown env config devdir`; ignore it.

A normal/TDM match that kills every red tank sets outcome to win and `stepWorld` stops. Base War ends only when a base is destroyed — simultaneous victories rule a draw, a 600s timeout resolves by base HP percent then surviving tanks — and emptying the enemy side changes nothing but who can still shoot. Headless Base War tests must expect a match to keep running after an elimination; other headless matches must leave one red alive. Respawns change what "every red tank" means: under MP rules a dead hull is only in the ground for `RESPAWN.delaySec`, so the wipe victory now needs the whole enemy company down in the same instant, and a hull killed one at a time never finishes anything. Tests that want a wipe still set `alive = false` directly — only `applyDamage` arms the countdown, so a hand-killed hull stays dead.

## Working agreements

- Do not commit unless asked. `src/api-key.local.ts` and `.env` are git-ignored now (`src/api-key.local.ts` was tracked from `8c7543a` to `fc88f21`, always with an empty placeholder; a fresh clone needs `cp src/api-key.local.example.ts src/api-key.local.ts` before `dev`/`build`, because `src/api-key.ts` imports it statically).
- Do not put the language model in the frame loop.
- Do not replace `planRoute` with a learned policy to fix a bad route. Draw the polyline and the danger field first.
- Do not give Needle final authority while it is slower and less confident than the phrase reader.
- MP bots hold until a player order names them. A new order rewrites only the tanks it names.
- Player moveTo and hold are not replaced by patrol or attack because an enemy is nearby. Shooting while staying on the path is allowed, but it is captioned as a deviation.
- Retreat exemptions are explicit `noRetreat`; ordered movement does not receive one implicitly.
- Anchors are free tiles the body can occupy, not wall centers.
- Typecheck stays clean.
- Barks are captioned text. No TTS or spoken bot audio into an open mic in v1.
- Eval and replay use a fixed timestep and a recorded RNG seed. The player's frame rate is never the source of truth for tests.
- No dodge without a perception model and reaction delay. A 0ms projectile dodge is a fairness regression.
- An order must never be silently dropped, retargeted, or overwritten. Every interrupt gets a reason code on the command record and a bark.
